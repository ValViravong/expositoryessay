Part 2

The introduction

The introduction works like a funnel. It starts wide to catch the reader, then gets narrower until it reaches your thesis statement, the last sentence. Tap each layer.

Five kinds of hook

All five examples introduce the same topic: air pollution in Thailand. Notice that a hook with a statistic or a quotation needs a citation too.

!

Careful with question hooks

A yes/no question is a weak hook. Look at "Have you ever heard of the moon conspiracy?" The reader can think "No" and stop reading. If you use a question, make it one the reader has to think about, and start answering it in the next sentence. A strong statement or statistic is usually the safer choice.

Part 3

The thesis statement

The thesis is the most important sentence in the essay. It states the purpose of the essay. It names the topic and gives your position. It can also list the main ideas you will develop.

Indirect or direct?

There are two kinds of thesis statement. Both give the topic and a position. The difference is whether the main points are named.

Indirect thesis points not named

Air pollution has become one of Thailand's most serious problems for several reasons.

  • Gives the topic and the position.
  • Says there are reasons, but does not say what they are.
  • The reader finds out the main points only in the topic sentences.

Direct thesis points named

Air pollution has become one of Thailand's most serious problems because it harms public health, damages the economy, and comes from sources that are difficult to control.

  • Gives the topic and the position.
  • Names all three main points, in the same order as the body paragraphs.
  • Works like a map: the reader knows the plan of the essay from the start.

Which should you use? For a five-paragraph essay, a direct thesis is usually clearer, because each main point leads straight to one body paragraph. The sample essay on this page uses a direct thesis.

The three requirements of a strong thesis

Direct or indirect, every strong thesis statement must meet all three of these requirements. If it breaks one, the whole essay becomes harder to write and to follow.

1 · One sentencea complete statement, not a question
2 · An opinionsomething people could agree or disagree with
3 · One ideaa single focus that keeps the essay together

1One complete sentence, not a question

✗The effects of alcohol.
✗Is alcohol a harmful substance?
✗There are some benefits from alcohol. However, there are also some disadvantages.
✔The legal drinking age for alcohol should be 21 due to the negative health effects and the impact on society.

2Give an opinion, not just a fact

A fact leaves nothing to explain. Someone should be able to agree or disagree with your thesis.

✗Cows produce milk.
✔The health benefits of cow's milk for adults should be reconsidered, because many people cannot digest it well and other foods provide the same nutrients.

3Focus on one idea

Two different ideas split the essay in half. It loses unity.

✗Social media harms the mental health of teenagers, and it has also changed the way companies advertise their products.
✔Social media harms the mental health of teenagers because it encourages unhealthy comparison, reduces sleep, and exposes them to online bullying.

Part 4

Body paragraphs

You write three body paragraphs, one for each main point in your thesis. They are the longest part of the essay. Every body paragraph follows the same recipe.

Topic sentenceStates the main idea of the paragraph. It matches one point in your thesis.
Supporting pointA smaller idea that supports the topic sentence.
Evidence + citationA fact, statistic or quotation from your research, with an APA in-text citation.
AnalysisYour critical thinking: what the evidence means and how it supports your point.
RepeatSupporting point → evidence → analysis, two or three times.
Concluding sentenceSums up the paragraph. Sometimes not necessary.

!

Do not repeat, do not drift

Use each piece of evidence once. Do not say the same fact again in new words to fill space. Every sentence must support the topic sentence. If a sentence is about something else, it is off-topic: delete it.

Part 5

Evidence and APA citations

Research evidence makes your paragraph believable. First, choose more than one reliable source for each body paragraph. Then use APA citations to show the reader where each piece of evidence came from.

Use two or more reliable sources in every body paragraph

One source gives you one point of view. When two or more reliable sources each add evidence to the same point, your paragraph becomes much harder to argue with. Aim for at least two different sources in every body paragraph.

Stronger proofTwo or more sources that support the same point show that your claim does not depend on one writer's opinion.
A fuller pictureEach source can add something different, for example the number of people affected and the cost.
Real researchYour reader can see that you read widely, not just the first article you found.
Less riskIf one source has an error or goes out of date, your whole paragraph does not fall apart.

What is a reliable source?

Ask four questions about every source.

  • Who wrote it? An expert, a university, a government office or a research centre.
  • When was it published? Recent enough for your topic. Statistics older than about five years may be out of date.
  • Where was it published? A journal, an official report or a trusted news organisation.
  • Does it show its evidence? It gives data, methods or its own references.

Use or avoid?

✔Peer-reviewed journal articles
✔Government, university and research-centre reports
✔Trusted news organisations that name their sources
✗Personal blogs, social media posts and videos
✗Websites that are selling a product
✗Answers from AI chatbots. They are not sources and can invent facts.
✗Wikipedia as a final source. Use it to start, then read and cite the original sources it links to.

!

One source is not enough

A body paragraph that cites the same source again and again is only as strong as that one source. Find a second reliable source that adds new evidence to the same point.

Two ways to cite

Use one of these for each piece of evidence, never both.

Narrative citation · author in the sentence

According to Ratanavalachai and Trivitayanurak (2023), expressway traffic caused about 32% of the yearly PM2.5 at one location in central Bangkok.

Write and between two authors in the sentence. The year goes in brackets right after the names.

Parenthetical citation · author in brackets

Expressway traffic caused about 32% of the yearly PM2.5 at one location in central Bangkok (Ratanavalachai & Trivitayanurak, 2023).

Write & inside brackets. The full stop goes after the closing bracket.

Quotation · exact words need a page number

One fact sheet warns that "particulate pollution is the biggest threat to life expectancy in Thailand" (Energy Policy Institute at the University of Chicago, 2025, p. 1).

Use quotations rarely, only when the exact words are powerful. Most evidence should be paraphrased: the idea in your own words, still with a citation. If a web source has no page number, give a paragraph number such as para. 4. For more rules, open the APA 7 Citation Lab.

Long group names · introduce the abbreviation once

The first time you cite a long organisation name, write it in full and add its abbreviation. After that, use only the abbreviation.

FirstNarrative: Energy Policy Institute at the University of Chicago (EPIC, 2025) states that…
FirstParenthetical: …in Thailand (Energy Policy Institute at the University of Chicago [EPIC], 2025).
LaterEvery later citation: (EPIC, 2025) or EPIC (2025)

Inside brackets, the abbreviation goes in square brackets, because you cannot put round brackets inside round brackets. In the References list, always write the full name.

!

No citation means plagiarism

If a fact, number or idea came from a source, it needs a citation, even if you changed the words. Only your own analysis and common knowledge can stand without one.

Part 6

The conclusion

The conclusion closes the essay. It reminds the reader of your thesis, sums up your three main points, and ends with a final thought. It is a complete paragraph, not one sentence.

Restated thesisYour thesis again, in new words. Do not copy it.
SummaryThe three main points, in one or two sentences.
Final thoughtYour last idea for the reader. Choose one or more types below.

Three weak conclusions

✗

In conclusion, air pollution is bad.

Only one sentence. A conclusion is a complete paragraph.

✗

In conclusion, smoke from neighbouring countries also makes up about 30% of the PM2.5 in northern Thailand (Inlaung et al., 2024).

New evidence and a citation. The conclusion is the only paragraph with no citations. Put all evidence in the body.

✗

In conclusion, air pollution has become one of Thailand's most serious problems because it harms public health, damages the economy, and comes from sources that are difficult to control.

The thesis copied word for word. Restate it in new words.

References

The References list starts on a new page. Every in-text citation in the essay matches one entry here, and every entry is cited in the essay.

References page basics

Look at the References page of the sample essay. It follows all seven rules.

  • New page Start the References list on a separate page after the conclusion.
  • Heading Write References at the top, centred and bold. Do not underline it, put it in italics or write "Works Cited".
  • A to Z order Order entries by the first author's family name, or by the first main word of the title if there is no author.
  • Double-spaced Double-space the whole page, inside and between entries.
  • Hanging indent The first line of each entry starts at the left margin. Every line after it is indented 0.5 in (1.27 cm).
  • Working links DOIs and URLs are full links that start with https://. Do not put a full stop after a link.
  • One-to-one match Every in-text citation has an entry, and every entry is cited in the essay.
